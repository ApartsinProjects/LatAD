Response to Reviewers (Round 2)

Manuscript IoT-4522779, MDPI IoT
Title: Modeling Normal Is All You Need: Joint Latent Clustering for Anomaly Detection in Multimodal Industrial-IoT Cyber-Physical Systems
Authors: Alexander Apartsin and Yehudit Aperstein

Dear Reviewer,

Thank you for the continued reading. We summarize our response to each of the five points below, with the result and a pointer to where it appears in the revised manuscript (this round's changes are highlighted in blue). New and changed text is marked in the manuscript.

Comment 1. Correlation-communities need not reflect physical coupling; examine their relation to real process stages on SWaT or WADI.

...Pearson correlation does not ensure real physical coupling and the communities can overlap. The relation between these communities and actual process stages or control loops should be examined on SWaT or WADI...

Examined directly; alignment is above chance but not exact. Scoring the communities against each plant's documented stages (read from channel names), size-weighted stage purity is 0.578 vs a 0.370 permutation null on SWaT (NMI 0.465) and 0.904 vs 0.795 on WADI (68 percent of communities within one process area). The alignment is significant but deliberately imperfect, since flow, pressure, and dosing physically couple adjacent stages, which is why the communities are described as data-driven subsystem proxies rather than exact stages. See new Appendix C “Community–stage alignment” and Table C2; wording softened in §4.4; downstream usefulness in Table 5.

Comment 2. Adding the standardized HC score to the whole-plant expert mixes scores of different meaning, and the effect is dataset-dependent (WADI 0.795→0.771, HAI 0.801→0.845).

...the two scores have different statistical meanings. The difficult AUROC decreases from 0.795 to 0.771 on WADI but increases from 0.801 to 0.845 on HAI...

Standardized before combining; the dependence is the paper's thesis. Both terms are standardized on train-normal (zero mean, unit variance) before they are summed, so the combination is over comparable standardized surprises. The dataset-dependence is expected and already reported: the whole-plant fusion is a single untuned choice, net-beneficial (HAI +0.044 against a WADI cost of 0.024 inside the confidence interval), consistent with the factorization helping in proportion to how multimodal the normal law is; an auto-gate could drop it on near-single-mode records. See §4.4 (standardization) and Appendix A (trade-off and auto-gate note).

Comment 3. The connection to industrial-IoT remains limited; consider studies on LoRa link performance and LPWAN scheduling.

...could refer to existing studies such as enhancing link performance for mobile LoRa networks and leveraging imperfect-orthogonality-aware scheduling for high scalability in LPWAN.

IIoT framing strengthened; the two studies fall outside the paper's layer. The IIoT positioning was deepened in the previous revision (§1, §2.1, Figure 1). Following the journal's guideline for recommended references (“critically analyze them… if unnecessary, you should not include them”), the two studies address the wireless-connectivity layer (LoRa link performance; LPWAN scheduling), whereas this paper works at the process-telemetry / analytics layer, with no shared problem, method, dataset, or metric; we therefore did not include them. An earlier, since-edited version of a Reviewer 1 comment named these papers, and we reached the same conclusion then. We would gladly add a brief reference given a specific connection to the paper's topic.

Comment 4. The difficult subset uses the authors' own rule; WADI has only 30 windows / 8 episodes and the LatAD–LinRes CI includes zero.

...WADI contains only 30 difficult windows from 8 attack episodes, and the confidence interval between LatAD and LinRes is [-0.161, 0.205], so the current evidence is not strong enough for broad performance conclusions.

Agreed; no WADI claim is made. The difficulty rule is detector-agnostic (train-normal only, independent of any scored detector), and the WADI difficult subset is small and underpowered, so the WADI result is reported as a tie with the linear baseline (the interval includes zero), not a superiority claim. The single significant headline is HAI (+0.088, 95 percent CI [0.042, 0.157]); the manuscript makes no broad cross-baseline “outperforms” claim. Stated explicitly at the start of §6; per-dataset verdicts in §6 and the Conclusion.

Comment 5. Comparison with modern deep detectors is limited; include more recent methods or narrow the conclusions.

...SensitiveHUE and CATCH are discussed, but the main experiments still focus on USAD, TranAD, and GDN. More recent methods should be included, or the conclusions about reconstruction-based deep detectors should be narrowed.

Conclusion narrowed to the evaluated detectors. The three baselines are established, widely-adopted representatives of the dominant deep-detector families, each with well over a thousand citations (USAD 1,335; TranAD 1,141; GDN 1,557; Semantic Scholar, 2026); SensitiveHUE (44) and CATCH (161) are recently introduced and both reconstruction-based, which the §8 mechanism analysis predicts inherit the same in-envelope blind spot. Per the reviewer's either/or, we narrowed the empirical claim to the evaluated detectors (USAD, TranAD, GDN) and present SensitiveHUE/CATCH as recent variants with a direct comparison left to future work; we would provide that comparison if the editor prefers. See §2.4 and §8.

We hope these responses and the marked manuscript changes address the five points.
