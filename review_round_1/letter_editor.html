Dear Academic Editor,

Thank you for handling our manuscript. This revision addresses every request, and it does so with substantial new experimental work rather than rewriting alone: the new experiments and datasets are listed first below, followed by a short answer to each of your requests. Full point-by-point detail is in the two accompanying Reviewer letters.

New experiments and datasets added in this revision

The following are new empirical results, not present in the submitted version:

  • Computational-cost benchmark (new Appendix B, Table B1): LatAD and the deep baselines trained and timed on one Modal A10G GPU plus a CPU edge proxy (parameters, training time, per-window latency, GPU and host memory).
  • Time-aware significance (Section 5.4, Figure 3): every claim recomputed with an episode-block bootstrap.
  • Source-of-gain ablation (new Table A4): cross-channel density versus a per-channel product, plus a raw-sequential-input variant and a richer temporal/spectral representation (Table A2).
  • Rare-regime imbalance sweep (new Appendix E, Tables E1–E2) on the newly added 3W oil-well dataset, which has genuinely imbalanced regime occupancy.
  • Between-regime overlap (A8) screen (new Appendix C, Table C1) across eight CPS sources, adding Cranfield, SKAB, MetroPT, SMD, and the Paderborn bearing testbed to WADI, HAI, and SWaT.
  • Multiscale (A9) and path-dependence (A10) tests (Section 8): a longer-window experiment, and three conditional scorers guarded by an order-shuffle control and a synthetic positive control.
  • GDN baseline run on all three streams under the identical protocol (Table 2, Figure 3), replacing the earlier inaccurate statement that GDN did not complete.
  • Subsystem-localization evaluation (new Table 5), and a synthetic mechanism illustration (new Figure A1).

Corrections

We re-audited every reported number against its source artifact. The WADI difficult subset was re-derived from 43 to 30 windows (a difficulty-stratification artifact), the rescaled WADI analyzer channel was dropped, and the canonical December-2015 SWaT release was adopted (Section 5.1); all tables were rebuilt. The main conclusions hold. The headline difficult-subset AUROC is HAI 0.845 and WADI 0.771; on the drift-dominated SWaT record the drift-aware typing of Section 7 recovers the difficult subset from 0.524 to 0.723.

Your direct requests

Please validate the assumptions that are currently unused (A3, A9, A10), provide direct experimental support for the rare-regime-safe nearest-component likelihood, report training and inference cost for edge deployment, use a time-aware statistical test, and add ablations isolating the source of the gain. 1. Validate the assumptions that are currently unused (A3, A9, A10)

New experiments. MIIM is now ten assumptions: seven realized and validated here (A1 to A7) and three specified for future work (A8 to A10). The unused "A3" is renumbered A8 and remeasured with a new observation-space screen (Appendix C); A9 (multiscale) is shown present and partly exploitable by a longer-window test; A10 (path dependence) shows no evidence under a guarded test (three conditional scorers, order-shuffle and synthetic positive controls). See Table 1 and Section 8; full analysis in the Reviewer 1 letter (comment 1).

2. Direct experimental support for the rare-regime-safe nearest-component likelihood

New experiment and dataset. The head is isolated in Table A5, and its rare-regime behavior is measured directly on the newly added 3W oil-well dataset (Appendix E, Tables E1–E2): at a matched false-alarm rate it removes the mixture's rare-regime false alarms and stays at or below three imbalance-aware alternatives at every occupancy threshold. Full detail in the Reviewer 1 letter (comment 2).

3. Report training and inference cost for edge deployment

New experiment. Appendix B and Table B1 report parameters, training time, per-window latency, and peak GPU and host memory for LatAD and the deep baselines, on one Modal A10G GPU plus a CPU edge proxy. LatAD-global trains in 3.2 / 16 / 3.5 s on WADI / HAI / SWaT (roughly 47 to 72 times faster than TranAD) and runs within gateway-class latency and about 19 to 29 MB of GPU memory. Full detail in the Reviewer 1 letter (comment 4).

4. Use a time-aware statistical test

New analysis. Every significance claim now uses an episode-block bootstrap that resamples whole attack episodes with contiguous normal blocks (Section 5.4). On the difficult subset: HAI is significant (+0.088, P < 0.0005); SWaT becomes significant after the drift-aware typing of Section 7 (+0.200, P = 0.014); WADI is ahead of every learned detector (each P < 0.05) and ties only the linear baseline (8 attack episodes). Full detail, with confidence intervals, in the Reviewer 1 letter (comment 5).

5. Add ablations isolating the source of the gain

New experiment. Table A4 replaces the cross-channel latent density with a per-channel product: the cross-channel term adds +0.200 on HAI, +0.017 on WADI, and −0.050 on SWaT. The source is therefore dataset-specific (HAI cross-channel density, WADI regime-community factorization, SWaT temporal and drift structure), not the window representation. Full detail in the Reviewer 1 letter (comment 6).
