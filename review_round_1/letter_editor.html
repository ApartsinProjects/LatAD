1. Validate the assumptions that are currently unused (A3, A9, A10)

Please validate the assumptions that are currently unused (A3, A9, A10) ...

Response. We agree, and have rescoped the contribution accordingly. We now present MIIM as ten assumptions of which seven are realized by the detector and validated here (A1 to A7) and three are specified for future work (A8 to A10). Table 1 lists the ten assumptions and their physical origin; Section 4 maps each realized assumption (A1 to A7) to the design section that implements it, and the caption states which assumptions are realized and which are future work.

On numbering: the between-regime assumption you refer to as "A3" (the one that motivated the basin-agreement head) is renumbered A8, so the seven realized instantaneous-window properties are contiguous A1 to A7. We remeasured it rather than only scoping it. The earlier apparent SKAB signal came from a cluster-responsibility screen that is blind by construction: on a synthetic overlap positive control (30 to 2 percent Bayes error) it rated the regimes as maximally crisp, and SKAB is the one screened dataset with no close-distinct regime pair at all (a single-loop 400-window rig; Appendix C, Table C1). Under valid observation-space metrics the A8 precondition (adjacent but distinct regimes) is present on nearly every real CPS dataset, but heavy mode overlap is absent; the masking form is detectable only with the trajectory (which couples A8 to A10), and the over-coverage form is already defended by the 80-component density head. A8 is therefore specified, partly defended, and verifiable at operational scale (Section 8, Appendix C).

A9 (multiscale structure) is present in the data as a measured property: per-channel autocorrelation timescales span 3.4 to 3.7 decades on all three plants, and a longer window adds about +0.06 difficult-subset AUROC on HAI, corroborating the +0.071 from explicit temporal features in Appendix A. A10 (path dependence) shows no evidence under these tests on these snapshot-detectable benchmarks by three conditional scorers, each validated against a passing synthetic positive control and an order-shuffle guard; "snapshot detectable" is thus now a measured finding, not an assumption (Table 1, Section 8).

2. Direct experimental support for the rare-regime-safe nearest-component likelihood

... provide direct experimental support for the rare-regime-safe nearest-component likelihood ...

Response. We agree the term needs direct support, and the imbalance sweep isolates its role. The nearest-component likelihood is isolated as a standalone score head in Table A5 (difficult-subset AUROC WADI 0.603, HAI 0.797, SWaT 0.479), beside the mixture density and the density-plus-nearest base, with its rationale stated as assumption A5 in Table 1. It is a rare-regime safety term: it keeps a valid window in a sparsely populated regime from being flagged for rarity alone, protecting the false-alarm rate rather than raising the aggregate score, so on a dataset without such rare-regime false alarms it can sit marginally below the density head alone (Table A5, WADI; Section 4.3 ii).

We now measure the imbalance behavior directly. Appendix E runs the comparison on the 3W oil-well dataset, which has genuinely imbalanced regime occupancy: at a matched false-alarm rate the nearest-component score removes the mixture's rare-regime false alarms (from 17, 17, 23 to 11, 9, 16 across three seeds). Sweeping the rare-occupancy threshold (2, 5, and 10 percent) against three imbalance-aware alternatives (a uniform-prior mixture, a tempered π0.5 mixture, and a kNN latent density), nearest-component is at or below every alternative at every threshold (at 5 percent, 0.148 versus 0.210 for the π-weighted mixture, with the prior-flattening alternatives in between; Table E2), so the benefit is specifically removal of the −log π rare-regime penalty, not generic imbalance-awareness. At the genuinely rare thresholds (5 percent occupancy and below) nearest never adds a rare-regime false alarm (0 of 30 cases per threshold); at a looser 10 percent threshold it can redistribute a few flags onto moderately-common tails (1 of 30). Difficult-subset detection is unchanged (true-positive rate at 5 percent false-alarm +0.007 ± 0.068). The property is defensive by design, and the single-mode benchmarks cannot exhibit it.

3. Report training and inference cost for edge deployment

... report training and inference cost for edge deployment ...

Response. Added; these are the numbers that matter for edge deployment. Appendix B and Table B1 report parameter count, training wall-clock, per-window inference latency, peak GPU memory, and host memory for LatAD (global and regime-community) and the deep baselines, measured on one Modal A10G GPU (24 GB) plus a Modal 8-vCPU CPU edge proxy, the same hardware for every method. LatAD-global trains in 3.2 / 16 / 3.5 s on WADI / HAI / SWaT, roughly 47 to 72 times faster than TranAD; per-window latency stays within gateway-class budgets (0.7 to 13 ms global, 17 to 32 ms for the full community stack), the CPU edge proxy scores a window in 0.6 to 14.6 ms (global), peak GPU memory is about 19 to 29 MB, and both configurations run within about 3.5 to 3.9 GB of host memory. The deployment paragraph in Section 8 no longer denies them.

4. Use a time-aware statistical test

... use a time-aware statistical test ...

Response. We agree this matters. Every significance statement now uses an episode-block bootstrap that resamples whole attack episodes together with contiguous normal blocks, applied uniformly to all three datasets and to every confidence interval and P-value (defined in Section 5.4; results in Section 6, Figure 3). The confidence interval is the two-sided 2.5 / 97.5 percentile of the paired difference; P is the one-sided bootstrap probability that the difference is at most zero. On the difficult subset:

  • HAI: significant, +0.088, 95 percent CI [0.042, 0.157], P < 0.0005.
  • SWaT (drift-dominated): the raw subset ties the strongest linear baseline (95 percent CI including zero); significant only after the drift-aware typing of Section 7 (+0.200, 95 percent CI [0.018, 0.355], P = 0.014).
  • WADI: significantly ahead of every learned detector (AutoEncoder, USAD, TranAD, Isolation Forest, and GDN, each cleared at P < 0.05 by the same per-detector episode-block bootstrap); ties only the linear baseline (+0.020, 95 percent CI [-0.161, 0.205], P = 0.46 over 8 attack episodes), not separable from it at that episode count.

The block length exceeds the window-overlap span, and the verdicts are unchanged when it is instead selected from the empirical serial dependence (the autocorrelation-decay length: 264 windows on HAI, 3 on WADI, 4 on SWaT) and under a seed-hierarchical resample over the five training seeds.

5. Add ablations isolating the source of the gain

... add ablations isolating the source of the gain ...

Response. Added as Table A4 (cited from Section 6), which replaces the cross-channel latent density with a channel-independent product of per-channel densities: the cross-channel density adds +0.200 on HAI, essentially nothing on the artifact-free WADI difficult subset (+0.017), whose difficult anomalies are single-channel or linear, and does not exceed the per-channel product on SWaT (-0.050). The head-level decomposition (Table A5), the factorization ladder (Table A1), and the representation check (Table A2, a smaller complementary lever) localize the same gain to the density heads and the regime-community factorization. The source is therefore dataset-specific: on HAI cross-channel density modeling (+0.200 over the per-channel product); on WADI the regime-community factorization; on SWaT the temporal and drift structure. In none of the three is the window representation the source.
