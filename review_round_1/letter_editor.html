Your direct requests

1. Validate the assumptions that are currently unused (A3, A9, A10)

Please validate the assumptions that are currently unused (A3, A9, A10) ...

Response. We reframed the MIIM specification from ten realized assumptions to ten assumptions of which seven are realized by the detector and validated here (A1 to A7) and three are specified for future work (A8 to A10). Table 1 is now grouped into these two blocks and carries a "Realized by" column mapping each assumption to the design section that implements it.

A note on numbering: the between-regime assumption the Reviewer and you refer to as "A3" (the one that motivated the basin-agreement head) has been renumbered A8, so that the seven realized instantaneous-window properties are contiguous A1 to A7. Its status was remeasured rather than simply scoped. The earlier apparent SKAB signal for it came from a cluster-responsibility screen that is blind by construction: on a synthetic overlap positive control (30 to 2 percent Bayes error) that screen rated the regimes as maximally crisp, and it also proved to be a variance-floor artifact of the estimator on SKAB's 400-window sample (Appendix C, Table C1). Under valid observation-space metrics the A8 precondition (adjacent but distinct regimes) is present on nearly every real CPS dataset, but heavy mode overlap is absent; the masking form is detectable only with the trajectory (which couples A8 to A10), and the over-coverage form is already defended by the 80-component density head. A8 is therefore specified, partly defended, and verifiable at operational scale (Section 7, Appendix C).

A9 (multiscale structure) is present in the data as a measured property: per-channel autocorrelation timescales span 3.4 to 3.7 decades on all three plants, and a longer window adds about +0.06 difficult-subset AUROC on HAI, corroborating the +0.072 from explicit temporal features in Appendix A. A10 (path dependence) is measured absent on these snapshot-detectable benchmarks by three conditional scorers, each validated against a passing synthetic positive control and an order-shuffle guard; "snapshot detectable" is thus now a measured finding, not an assumption (Table 1, Section 7).

2. Direct experimental support for the rare-regime-safe nearest-component likelihood

... provide direct experimental support for the rare-regime-safe nearest-component likelihood ...

Response. The nearest-component likelihood is isolated as a standalone score head in Table 6 (difficult-subset AUROC WADI 0.603, HAI 0.797, SWaT 0.723), alongside the mixture density and the density-plus-nearest base, and its design rationale is stated as assumption A5 in Table 1. It is a rare-regime safety term: its role is to keep a valid window in a sparsely populated regime from being flagged for rarity alone, protecting the false-alarm rate rather than raising the aggregate score, and on a dataset without such rare-regime false alarms it can sit marginally below the density head alone (Table 6, WADI; Section 4.3 ii). Its distinctive benefit over the mixture NLL, and the rare-regime false-positive question, would surface under the operating-regime imbalance sweep you propose. We did not run that imbalance sweep, and we support the head here with the Table 6 head-level isolation and the A5 design argument rather than assert a benefit we did not measure.

3. Report training and inference cost for edge deployment

... report training and inference cost for edge deployment ...

Response. Added. Appendix B and Table B1 report parameter count, training wall-clock, per-window inference latency, peak GPU memory, and host memory for LatAD (global and regime-community) and the deep baselines, measured on one Modal A10G GPU (24 GB) plus a Modal 8-vCPU CPU edge proxy, the same hardware for every method. LatAD-global trains in 3.2 / 16 / 3.5 s on WADI / HAI / SWaT, roughly 47 to 72 times faster than TranAD; per-window latency stays within gateway-class budgets (0.7 to 13 ms global, 17 to 32 ms for the full community stack), a CPU edge proxy scores a window in 0.6 to 14.6 ms (global), peak GPU memory is tens of MB (about 20 to 29 MB for LatAD), and both configurations run within about 3.5 to 3.9 GB of host memory. These cost measurements are added in Appendix B and Table B1, and the deployment paragraph in Section 7 is reworded so it no longer denies them.

4. Use a time-aware statistical test

... use a time-aware statistical test ...

Response. Every significance statement is on an episode-block bootstrap that resamples whole attack episodes together with contiguous normal blocks, applied uniformly to all three datasets and to every reported confidence interval and P-value (defined in Section 5.4; results in Section 6, Table 4, Figure 3). The confidence interval is the two-sided 2.5 / 97.5 percentile of the paired difference and P is the one-sided bootstrap probability that the difference is at most zero. On the difficult subset the margin is significant on HAI (+0.088, 95 percent CI [0.042, 0.157], P about 0) and canonical SWaT (+0.055, 95 percent CI [0.012, 0.104], P = 0.007); on WADI LatAD is significantly ahead of every learned detector (the AutoEncoder, USAD, TranAD, Isolation Forest, and GDN, each cleared at P < 0.05 by the same per-detector episode-block bootstrap), and ties only the linear baseline (+0.020, 95 percent CI [-0.161, 0.205], P = 0.46 over 8 attack episodes), where we state plainly that WADI is not separable from the linear baseline at that episode count.

5. Add ablations isolating the source of the gain

... add ablations isolating the source of the gain ...

Response. Added as a main-text table. Table 5 (Section 6) replaces the cross-channel latent density with a channel-independent product of per-channel densities: the cross-channel density adds +0.200 on HAI and +0.141 on canonical SWaT over the channel-independent baseline, the two datasets where LatAD wins significantly, and essentially nothing on the artifact-free WADI difficult subset (+0.017), whose difficult anomalies are single-channel or linear. The head-level decomposition (Table 6) and the factorization ladder (Table A1) localize the same gain to the density heads and the regime-community factorization, and the representation check (Table A2) shows the window representation is a smaller, complementary lever. The gain is therefore cross-channel density modeling, not the window representation.
